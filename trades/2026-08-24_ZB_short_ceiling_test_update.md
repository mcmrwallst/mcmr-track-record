## The read, in short

* **The fix is too small for the problem.** $4bn per operation is a maturity swap funded inside
the same borrowing envelope — not new money, not QE — against a debt stock that crossed **$40
trillion** the day before. It bought two sessions.
* **This was the thesis test, and the thesis passed.** The 2026-07-22 view says this yield rise is
driven by supply and policy, not by a growth scare — so there is no flight-to-quality bid to
stop it. *(Plainly: a yield rise caused by panic elsewhere gets halted when investors run into
Treasuries for safety. That brake cannot engage when Treasuries are themselves the source of the
stress — nobody runs into the thing they are running from.)* Treasury tried to supply that
missing bid by hand. It lasted 48 hours.
* **Dollar: weaker now, firmer later.** The announcement cut **DXY 0.8% to 99** — the same shape
as the yen intervention three weeks earlier, which has already faded. I expect USD/JPY to resume
higher on the rate differential and other factors not explored here.
* **The pairing to watch:** dollar strength *and* long-end weakness at the same time. That is not
the usual combination, and it is the specific thing I am tracking from here.
* **HY ties in.** At **\~275bp** spreads are historically tight. I expect widening, and in most
macro regimes HY widening coincides with a stronger dollar — which supports both the dollar leg
above and the term-premium view underneath this position.
* **The real risk is not the buyback.** It is the **fiscal consolidation plan due 08-21 → 08-28**,
not delivered as of this commit. Term premium is compensation for fiscal risk; a plan the market
finds credible compresses it directly.
* **Falsification:** if the 30-year sustains back below the **4.7–5.0%** band it just broke, the
break was false and I am wrong — not early, wrong.

## Reasoning in full

Reviewed the position after the buyback announcement and I am keeping it. Treasury moved the long
end for two sessions and the market took it straight back, which is the cleanest test of the
thesis I could have asked for and did not have to pay for. An announced official bid at the long
end is the closest thing to a circuit breaker this market has, and it failed inside 48 hours
against a debt stock that had just crossed $40 trillion months ahead of schedule.

The currency leg is the second read. The announcement knocked DXY to 99 and pulled USD/JPY down
with it, echoing the early-August intervention that ran \~164 to \~155 and has already surrendered
roughly a third of its gain. Both moves share a structure: an official action produces a sharp
move, then fundamentals reassert. If the yen resumes weakening, the dollar firms on that leg while
the Treasury market stays under pressure — and dollar strength alongside long-end weakness is the
unusual configuration this position is really expressing. The HY note from earlier sits underneath
that: spreads at \~275bp leave far more room to widen than to compress, and HY widening has usually
run with a firmer dollar.

full thesis still stands, including the inflation leg, which remains a second engine.

Two things logged before the fact rather than after. The fiscal consolidation announcement is a
larger threat to this position than the buyback ever was, because it attacks the reason for the
price rather than the price itself. And the falsification level above stands as written, so it
cannot be renegotiated later.

Exit criteria unchanged from entry: 110'26'0 as a hard working order, plus the discretionary
technical signal defined pre-entry. No averaging down, no thesis-morphing, no stop widening. Fill
