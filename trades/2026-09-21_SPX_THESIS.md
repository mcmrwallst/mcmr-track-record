## Thesis

The index is priced for a continuation that the policy and credit configuration does not support. I am short the S\&P on the view that the current setup replicates the *preconditions* of early October 2018 — a market within touching distance of a record, on compressed volatility, with a central bank explicitly tightening and signalling more, while the rate-sensitive parts of the market have already turned.

The trade is a bet on multiple compression, not on an earnings collapse. In Q4 2018 the S\&P fell roughly 20% in a year when earnings grew around 20%. Every point of that drawdown came out of the multiple. That is the shape I am positioning for.

\---

## The analog

The reference window is **late September to early October 2018** — specifically the period *before* the break, not the break itself. On 3 October 2018 the S\&P was fractions off its record, ISM services had printed the highest reading in the survey's history, unemployment was about to hit a 50-year low, and volatility was near the lows. The market topped on the best economic news of the cycle, because that data was what forced the Fed's hand.

The transferable lesson is that **the bearish catalyst was strong data, not weak data.** That is the lens I am applying now.

### Condition-by-condition

**1. Credit is priced for perfection.**
Investment grade spreads are at their tightest since 1998; high yield is at 2007 tights. Nothing adverse is discounted. This is the same starting condition as October 2018, when high yield sat near its post-crisis tight — two months before the high yield primary market shut completely and priced zero deals in December, the first such month since November 2008. Tight spreads are not a bearish signal on their own. They are a statement about asymmetry: there is no room for credit to improve and a great deal of room for it to deteriorate.

**2. Policy is tightening, and the market is pricing more of it.**
The Fed has resumed hiking and has guided to further increases this year. SOFR futures price additional tightening ahead rather than an imminent turn. This is the 2018 condition — a central bank raising rates into an expensive market and telling the market in advance that it is not finished. The 2018 equivalent was explicit: rates were "a long way from neutral."

**3. Long yields are at cycle-relevant levels.**
The 10-year is at the top of its range and has been the dominant driver of the index multiple. In 2018 the break came when the 10-year moved from 3.05% to 3.26% in four sessions and the long-duration complex repriced. The level differs; the function is identical. The discount rate is the marginal driver of the index here, not earnings.

**4. Breadth has already diverged — and the divergence is wider than it was in 2018.**

This is the condition carrying the most weight in the trade, so I am showing the numbers rather than asserting the pattern.

**Early October 2018, measured at 3 October — the week before the break:**

||Level|High|From high|
|-|-|-|-|
|S\&P 500|2,925.51|2,930.75 (20 Sep)|**−0.18%**|
|Russell 2000|1,668.60|1,742.80 (4 Sep)|**−4.26%**|

Spread: **4.08 percentage points.** The S\&P was effectively at its record. Small caps were already more than four per cent below theirs, and had topped roughly two weeks earlier. Nine weeks later the S\&P was down 19.8%.

**Today, 21 September 2026:**

||Level|High|From high|
|-|-|-|-|
|S\&P 500|7,774.55|August 2026|**within 1%**|
|Russell 2000|2,884.00|3,069.80 (16 Aug)|**−6.05%**|

Spread: **over 5 percentage points** — wider than the 2018 reading on any reasonable measure of where the August S\&P record sits.

The *shape* of the divergence differs and I want that on the record rather than smoothed over. In 2018 the Russell topped roughly a fortnight ahead of the S\&P; the tell was a leading peak. In 2026 both indices peaked in August, and the divergence has opened up on the way back: **the S\&P has recovered its August drawdown and returned to its high, and the Russell has not — it is six per cent below and has not participated in the rally.**

I regard the 2026 version as the stronger signal, not the weaker one. A leading peak can be noise. A failure to participate in a five-week recovery to record highs is a statement about where the marginal buyer is willing to go, and it is concentrated in exactly the cohort that feels funding costs first — small caps carrying floating-rate debt into a central bank that has resumed hiking and is guiding to more.

The index makes its high last. That is the mechanism I am trading.

**5. Volatility is compressed.**
The VIX is in the mid-teens against a market near a record, with a hiking central bank, an unresolved oil shock and a trade summit pending. Stated plainly as a condition: it means the price of downside is low relative to the distribution of outcomes. **It is not the justification for this trade** — see the exclusion below.

**6. Earnings and consensus are in the same posture.**
Earnings growth is still positive and the consensus view remains constructive, exactly as in the autumn of 2018 when Q3 earnings were tracking above 25% growth and the strength of the economy was read as a reason to own equities. Consensus and earnings are not carrying bearish information here; nor were they in 2018.

**7. Oil is elevated.**
Crude is high on supply and geopolitical risk. In 2018 oil was collapsing through Q4 — the opposite sign, and the reason the Fed had room to pivot in January 2019. This is a genuine difference and I treat it as one. Elevated oil into a tightening cycle removes the disinflationary release valve that ended the 2018 drawdown.

## The transmission channel: anticipated AI capital expenditure slowdown

This is the part of the thesis that ties the equity position to the rates behaviour, and it is the reason I think the current correlation regime is unstable.

**The set-up.** AI-related capital expenditure is running at roughly $700bn a year across the large platforms, up around three-quarters year on year, and accounts for something close to half of S\&P 500 earnings growth. It is increasingly funded with debt rather than purely from operating cash flow, and the depreciation associated with it is still largely ahead of the reported numbers rather than in them.

That spending is doing two jobs at once, and this is the point that matters:

1. It is the **earnings engine of the index** — the growth that justifies the multiple.
2. It is also a material block of **aggregate investment demand** — data centres, power, construction, semiconductors, the supply chains beneath them.

**The channel.** Markets price anticipation, not realisation. If the market begins to discount a slowdown in that capital expenditure — whether because funding costs at a 5% long end make debt-financed buildout uneconomic, because the depreciation charge starts landing, or because return-on-investment scepticism sets in — then both jobs fail at the same time:

* **Equities:** the growth engine deflates, in the exact cohort that constitutes the index. Multiple compression and earnings revision arrive together, concentrated rather than diffuse.
* **Rates:** a large block of private investment demand is withdrawn from the economy. Growth expectations fall. Long yields decline through the **growth** channel.

**This is how the correlation flips.** For most of this year, equities and long bonds have sold off together, because the dominant driver has been fiscal supply and term premium — an inflation and supply story, under which both assets lose. A capital expenditure slowdown is a *demand* story. Under a demand story the classic negative correlation reasserts itself: equities down, bonds up. One event, opposite signs, and it dominates the supply channel that has been driving them together.

**Why I think the breadth divergence may already be an early read on this.** A meaningful share of the Russell 2000's strength this year came from AI supply-chain names — the smaller chip, equipment and infrastructure suppliers levered to the buildout rather than competing with the leaders. Those are the businesses that feel a financing squeeze and an order slowdown first, and they carry floating-rate debt into a hiking cycle. The Russell sitting six per cent below its August high while the S\&P returns to record territory is consistent with the capex-exposed periphery turning before the capex-funded core. The 2018 analogue is exact in structure: semiconductors peaked in March 2018, six months before the index, because the capital expenditure cohort always turns first.

**What would confirm or kill this.** Q3 platform earnings and the capital expenditure guidance that comes with them, late October into November. The specific thing I am watching is not the headline number but **how it is funded** — capital expenditure financed from operating cash flow is sustainable at a 5% long end; capital expenditure financed with debt at a 5% long end is where a credit widening reaches index earnings. If that guidance is raised and cash-funded, this leg of the thesis is wrong and the read is bullish.

**Status of this section.** No platform has guided capital expenditure down. This is an argument about **anticipation** and about what a change in anticipation would do — not a claim that a slowdown is underway. Stated that way deliberately so it cannot be reread later as a call I did not make.

## The mechanism I am watching — stated as a hypothesis, not a conclusion

The chain I think is plausible: an elevated oil price that originates as a **supply** shock eventually transmits into **demand** destruction, and demand destruction is a slowdown signal. If that transition happens, long yields would be expected to fall as growth expectations reprice, even with the supply and term-premium pressure that has been driving them higher.

**I have not done the work to support this yet and I am not trading it.** It is written down here so that it is on the record as an open hypothesis with a date attached, and so that if it develops I cannot claim afterwards to have had it. The equity short does not depend on it. If the supply-to-demand transition never occurs, the thesis above stands or falls on its own conditions.

## What this expression is explicitly NOT justified on

I have previously pre-registered that *"further volatility compression means wider asymmetry"* is the short-seller's standard error, and that any expression justified on that basis alone is invalid at entry.

This expression is not justified on that basis. Compressed volatility appears above as one condition among seven, and it is the condition carrying the least weight. The load is carried by the policy configuration, the credit starting point and the breadth divergence. If those three were absent, a low VIX alone would not have produced this trade.

## Timing

The macro conditions above define the direction and the hold. They do not define the entry date.

For the entry itself I used technical analysis, in a **minority weight**, for timing only. It is not part of the thesis and it is not a reason to stay in the position. If the macro conditions break, the technical picture does not override them; if the technical picture breaks and the macro conditions hold, the stop is where the argument ends.

## Invalidation

The position is wrong, and the thesis is wrong, if:

1. **Price:** 7861.50 trades. Hard stop, no discretion.
2. **Credit refuses to confirm.** If high yield spreads remain at or below current tights while the index falls, the mechanism I have described is not the mechanism operating, and I am short for the wrong reason even if I am making money.
3. **The breadth divergence closes.** If the Russell recovers its high and leads the index higher, the single tell I am leaning on has failed, and the 2018 structure is not present.
4. **Policy turns.** A credible signal that the hiking path has ended removes the central condition.

## Forward note

If the correlation regime shifts during the life of this position — specifically if bonds begin to rally on growth-scare repricing rather than selling off on supply and term premium — I may take a long position in bonds on value. That would be a **separate thesis with its own entry, stop and record**, not an extension of this one and not a hedge against it. Noted in advance so that if it happens it is a planned trade rather than a reaction.

The capital expenditure channel above is my candidate explanation for what would cause that flip. If it plays out, the same event that pays the equity short is what makes the bond long available — which means the two positions would be sequential expressions of one regime call, not a diversified book. I am recording that now so that I size the second trade accordingly if it arrives, rather than treating it as independent risk.

## Open research

1. Quantify the supply-shock-to-demand-destruction transmission properly. At what sustained crude level, and over what lag, does it show in demand data.
2. Establish what proportion of current AI-related capital expenditure is funded from cash flow versus debt at the margin, and identify the spread level at which that funding breaks. This is the specific channel through which a credit widening would reach index earnings, and it is the single highest-value item on this list.
3. Quantify the share of AI-related capital expenditure that lands in measurable investment demand, so the rates leg of the correlation-flip argument rests on a number rather than on a description.
4. Build the historical distribution of small-cap-versus-large-cap divergence at index tops, to establish whether the 2018 pattern generalises or whether I have selected a single instance that fits.
