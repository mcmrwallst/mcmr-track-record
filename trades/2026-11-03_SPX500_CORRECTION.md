## The correlation flip

**Convention:** daily S\&P 500 returns against daily Treasury *price* returns. Positive means stocks and bonds move together (the supply / term-premium regime). Negative means bonds hedge stocks (the demand / growth regime).

The thesis stated: *"equities and long bonds have sold off together… Under a demand story the classic negative correlation reasserts itself."*

|Window|9 Oct|29 Oct|
|-|-|-|
|20-day|+0.51|**−0.46**|
|60-day|+0.45|**+0.18** (2026 low)|
|1-year|+0.32|+0.32|

The 20-day has flipped deeply negative and the 60-day has moved to near neutral. That is the direction and channel the thesis predicted. Real yields carried the move: the real 10-year fell from 2.91% to 2.63% and the 2-year from 4.80% to 4.61%, with breakevens flat at 2.33–2.35%. That is growth repricing, not inflation.

**Not yet a regime.** 20-day negative spells reverted in Nov 2025 and Feb–Mar 2026. I count the regime as shifted only when **both the 20-day and the 60-day are below zero.**

## Q3 checkpoint: scored

The test as written on 21 Sep: *if capex guidance is "raised and cash-funded, this leg of the thesis is wrong."*

* **Guidance was raised, not cut.** Alphabet guides $195–205bn for 2026, Meta $135–145bn and Amazon about $220bn. None signals a 2027 slowdown.
* **It is not cash-funded at the margin.**

  * Alphabet: Q3 free cash flow −$9.1bn; long-term debt $46.5bn → $125.1bn this year.
  * Meta: Q3 free cash flow −$2.1bn, its first negative quarter in over a decade; about $35bn of new equity launched on 26 Oct.
  * Amazon: trailing twelve-month free cash flow −$42.4bn.
  * Combined Q3 capex exceeded operating cash flow by about $26bn.

**Verdict: the kill condition did not fire.** The spending is funded with debt and equity, which is the route the thesis named for credit widening to reach index earnings.

**Limit:** no platform has cut capex, and realised spending is still accelerating. The market is discounting a slowdown, not reporting one.
