# Reporting methodology

**Version:** 1.0 · **Applies to:** `PERFORMANCE.md` and everything in
`/performance` · **Change log:** at the end of this file

This document fixes how the record is counted, valued and reported *before*
any report is built from it. Rules written after the numbers are known can be
chosen to flatter them, so this file was committed and timestamped on its own,
before the first report it governs.

**How this file changes.** It is edited in place, and every edit follows three
rules:

1. **Logged.** Each change gets a new version number (1.1, 1.2, …) and an entry
   in the change log below, with the date, what changed and why. The file's
   full history is also visible in git.
2. **Forward only.** A change applies only to reports generated after it is
   committed. Frozen reports keep the version they were built under and are
   never rebuilt under a newer one, even when the change corrects a mistake.
3. **Cited.** Every report states the methodology version and the commit it
   was built under.

A correction that would make a past result look better is never applied to
that result (§11).

---

## 1. What this record is

**This is a model portfolio: the reference book for my trading.** Every
position is a decision published in real time and timestamped in this
repository before its outcome is known. Live execution follows the same theses,
though sizing, timing and instruments can differ, and execution records are not
published here. The results reported are the reference book's results.

## 2. Units

**R.** 1R is the loss the position would take at its stop, as published in the
OPEN file. −1.00R is a full stop-out.

**Percentage.** Each position's risk is published as a percentage of model
equity (for example, *1R = 2.02% of equity*). A result in percent is the R
result multiplied by that figure. Where a trade file states a percentage, that
figure is used. Where a file states only R, the percentage is derived from the
published 1R% and marked *derived*. Where neither exists, the position appears
as a gap and is never estimated.

**Model equity** is notional. It is reported only as an index (section 7),
never as an amount of money.

## 3. Trades and theses

- **Event:** one file in `/trades` (open, add, reduce, update, correction, exit,
  conclusion).
- **Trade:** every event from one OPEN to the EXIT that fully closes it, with a
  permanent ID (`T001`, `T002`, …) in `performance/registry.csv`.
- **Thesis:** the idea a trade expresses, with a permanent ID (`TH01`, …). A
  thesis can hold several trades; a trade belongs to exactly one thesis.
- Switching instruments on the same view with no gap in exposure (closing one
  and opening the other at the same time) continues the **same trade**.
  Re-entering after a full exit, including after a stop-out, is a **new
  trade** under the same thesis. Example: the ZN stop-out on 4 Aug and the ZB
  entry on 5 Aug are two trades under one thesis.
- IDs are assigned when the OPEN is published and are never reassigned.
- **Nothing is excluded.** Files that are not trades (for example,
  `2026-07-22_ES_UPDATE_EXAMPLE.md`) are listed in the registry as `non-trade`,
  so their exclusion is visible.

## 4. Model fills

Because no position is executed, every price must be one a reader can check:

- Each trade file names the exact instrument modelled (for example, *ES
  front-month future* or *S&P 500 cash index*).
- **Every entry and exit price must have traded** in that instrument at the
  stated time, checkable against public intraday data.
- **Stops fill at the stop price.** If the market gaps through the stop, the
  fill is the first traded price beyond it, which is the less flattering
  figure.
- If a file gives no time for a fill, the price must have traded between the
  previous push receipt and the push receipt of that file. Otherwise the fill is
  marked *unverifiable*.

## 5. Time

- All times are **UTC**.
- **Months** run from 1st 00:00 to the 1st of the next month 00:00.
  **Quarters** and **years** are calendar quarters and years, start inclusive
  and end exclusive.
- **Inception** is 22 Jul 2026, the first published position. July 2026 is a
  partial month and is labelled as one.

## 6. Valuing open positions

At each month-end, every open position is marked at the **last official close
on or before the month-end**:

| Instrument | Marking price |
|---|---|
| ZN, ZB and other CME futures | CME daily settlement price |
| ES | CME daily settlement price |
| S&P 500 cash index / SPX500 | S&P 500 official close |

Open R = (mark − published entry) ÷ published stop distance, signed for
direction. Open % = open R × the position's 1R%.

## 7. Period results

- **A period's result** is realised results in the period plus the change in
  open marks during the period. A position held across a month-end contributes
  to both months, and its parts sum to its full result.
- **Within a month,** results are added. **Across months,** returns are
  compounded: (1 + r₁) × (1 + r₂) × … − 1. Quarters and years are compounded
  from their months.
- Adding results within a month treats each position's percentage as if it
  shared one equity base. At the sizes in this record the difference is small;
  it is stated here rather than hidden.
- **The index** starts at 100 at inception and moves with the monthly returns.
  **Drawdown** is the percentage the index stands below its previous peak, at
  each month-end.

## 8. Statistics

Every statistic is shown with its sample size (n). All are calculated from
month-end values or closed trades, as stated.

**Return and drawdown (in %, month-end):**

| Statistic | Definition |
|---|---|
| Since inception, year to date | compounded from monthly returns |
| Maximum drawdown (MDD) | largest fall of the index from a previous peak, with peak and trough months |
| Current drawdown | index ÷ highest index to date − 1 |
| Longest time under water | most consecutive month-ends below a previous peak; *ongoing* if not yet recovered |
| Best month, worst month | highest and lowest monthly return |
| Positive months | months with a return above zero ÷ all months |
| Return ÷ MDD | since-inception return ÷ maximum drawdown; **not annualised** |

**Trades (in R, closed trades only):**

| Statistic | Definition |
|---|---|
| Win rate | wins ÷ closed trades (win > +0.10R, loss < −0.10R, scratch in between) |
| Average win, average loss, payoff ratio | mean R of wins; mean R of losses; average win ÷ \|average loss\| |
| Expectancy | total R ÷ closed trades |
| Profit factor | sum of winning R ÷ \|sum of losing R\| |
| Best-trade share | best trade's R ÷ total R |
| Holding period | average days held, winners and losers separately |
| Longest losing streak | most consecutive closed trades that were losses |

**Risk taken (from published figures):** average and largest risk per trade
(1R as % of model equity), and largest published notional exposure.

**Shown only once enough data exists.** The following appear automatically
once the record has **24 monthly returns**, and not before, because a shorter
sample cannot support them: annualised return and volatility, Sharpe ratio
(risk-free rate: 3-month US Treasury bill), Sortino ratio, and the correlation
of monthly returns with the S&P 500. Until then, the page states that they are
withheld and why.

## 9. Thesis verdicts

Each thesis carries a verdict, kept **separate from its result**:
*Confirmed*, the stated driver played out; *Killed*, a pre-registered kill
condition fired; *Untested*, the trade closed before the thesis could be
judged; *Open*, not yet judged.

A verdict comes only from an EXIT or CONCLUSION file, and the report links to
the passage. A confirmed thesis with a losing trade, or a killed thesis with a
winning one, is reported as exactly that.

**Two hit rates, never merged:**

| Statistic | Counts | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Trade win rate | closed trades | wins ÷ closed trades (§8) |
| Thesis hit rate | judged theses | Confirmed ÷ (Confirmed + Killed). *Untested* and *Open* are excluded from both parts and their count is shown beside the rate |

**Thesis × trade grid.** Every closed trade is placed in one cell, using its
own result and its thesis's verdict:

|  | Trade won | Trade lost |
|---|---|---|
| **Thesis confirmed** | right and paid | right idea, wrong timing or sizing |
| **Thesis killed** | wrong idea, got lucky | wrong and paid for it |
| **Untested / open** | counted separately | counted separately |

The grid is shown with counts, and each cell lists its trade IDs.

## 10. Freezing reports

- Monthly, quarterly and annual reports are generated by a scheduled run on the
  1st of each month (UTC). Each gets a timestamp proof and is **never
  regenerated**.
- Periods that ended before this file was committed are calculated afterwards
  from public closing prices. They are labelled **backfilled on [date]**, not
  presented as frozen at the time.
- An error found later is corrected by a dated notice beside the report, not by
  rebuilding it.
- `PERFORMANCE.md` and thesis pages are live and regenerate on every push.

## 11. Corrections

A correction may only move a published result **towards the less flattering
figure**. If a correction would improve a result, the original stands.
Precedent: the ZN stop correction of 27 Jul 2026 said the trade would be
reported at −0.49R; the exit reported −1.00R against the risk actually
published (4.00% of equity). The harsher figure is the one used.

## 12. Costs

Results are **gross**. Transaction costs are assumed to be negligible: one tick
of spread on ES is about 0.004% of notional, roughly 0.01% of equity per side
even at 350% notional. Futures carry is not modelled. It is about the size of
the spread or larger over multi-week holds, and has favoured short positions in
this record.

## 13. Charts

Every chart is generated by script from `performance/registry.csv` and the marking
prices, and is committed with its data. Points are month-end only, so moves
within a month do not appear. The cumulative chart shows the full history from
inception on one curve and is never cut by year.

The charts are:

1. **Index:** growth of 100 since inception.
2. **Drawdown:** percentage below the previous peak at each month-end.
3. **Monthly returns:** one bar per month.
4. **Result per trade:** R of every closed trade, in the order closed.
5. **Risk per trade:** published 1R% at entry for every trade, marked by
   result, so sizing can be judged against outcomes.
